What Is Coir?

A renewable material, not a raw resource we deplete

Coir is the fibrous layer between the hard shell and the outer skin of a coconut. It reaches us as a by-product — the husk that remains once the coconut has been harvested for its water, flesh and oil.

Because no tree is grown or felled for coir alone, it is one of the most genuinely sustainable growing media available — and every part of the husk is put to use.

From One Husk

Our raw-material families

1 · Cocopeat / Coir Pith

The spongy pith between the fibres — the core of coir growing media, prized for its high water-holding capacity. Supplied in three grades.

Natural Coir Pith

Raw, unwashed pith straight from the husk — maximum water retention, used where salts are managed or flushed.

Screened Coir Pith

Sieved and graded to a consistent particle size — clean, uniform and ready for blending.

Crushed Coir Pith

Finely crushed pith for dense, high-capacity water-holding media and compressed products.

Information only

2 · Coconut Husk Chips

Cut pieces of husk prized for aeration — ideal for orchids, anthuriums and free-draining hydroponic blends. Available in graded sizes.

4S – Small Chips

Small-grade (4S) chips that add air porosity to a blend while still holding moisture.

7 mm / 10 mm / 20 mm

Graded chip sizes for custom aeration — larger chips for orchids and fast-draining systems.

Information only

3 · Short Fibers

Short lengths of coir fibre that reinforce blends, improve structure and aid drainage. Supplied in two length grades.

0 – 10 mm

Short fibres up to 10 mm — reinforce blends and improve structure and drainage.

0 – 15 mm

Short fibres up to 15 mm — extra structural body for moulded and compressed products.

Information only
